Theoretical Framework

Two Hemispheres, One Mind?

How does the human brain construct a unified conscious experience when its processing pipelines are strictly lateralized? This digital lab compares two landmark scientific pathways:

01

Roland et al. (2017) • Physiological

Using rs-fMRI to map resting-state networks before and after surgical bisection. Proves the causal role of the Corpus Callosum in maintaining functional connectivity.

02

Michael S. Gazzaniga (2000) • Cognitive

Synthesizing 40 years of split-brain patient behavioral studies. Proposes the Corpus Callosum as the great evolutionary “enabler” of human cognitive specialization.

Physiological Explanation:

In normal brains, trying to draw a circle and a square simultaneously results in massive spatial interference—the motor plans cross over the corpus callosum and fuse. In split-brain patients, because the hemispheres are disconnected, each hand carries out its own independent spatial program without cross-talk!

The Interpreter at Large: Anosognosia & Reduplicative Paramnesia

Gazzaniga explains that neurological syndromes like anosognosia (denying paralysis of a left hand) represent the intact left-hemisphere interpreter trying to maintain a coherent narrative. Lacking feedback due to a right-parietal lesion, it constructs the logical explanation: “That hand on my lap is not mine, it must belong to you!”

Physiological Focus

Roland et al. (2017) Summary

A cohort study of 22 patients with medically refractory epilepsy undergoing complete (16 patients) or partial (6 patients) corpus callosotomy. Resting-state networks were evaluated before and after bisection.

High-Yield Takeaways:

1. Multimodal Associative Vulnerability

Homotopic functional connectivity dropped to near zero in multimodal regions (frontal, parietal, temporal), whilst primary visual and sensorimotor regions were partially spared.

2. Splenium as a Functional Hub

In partial callosotomy, sparing posterior splenial fibers preserved functional visual connectivity and even helped support frontal homotopic connectivity via polysynaptic pathways.

3. Absence of Postnatal Recovery

Multiyear longitudinal follow-up (ranging from 2 to 7 years) showed no evidence of recovery of interhemispheric functional connectivity, implying that critical compensatory windows occur only during prenatal or very early postnatal development.

Cognitive Focus

Gazzaniga (2000) Summary

Synthesis of 40 years of bisection studies in split-brain subjects (including key patients such as L.B., N.G., J.W., and D.R.). Focuses on lateralization, sensory-motor coupling, attention, and consciousness.

High-Yield Takeaways:

1. The Evolutionary Enabler Hypothesis

Sparing of redundant functions (e.g., visuospatial processing in the right hemisphere) allowed the left hemisphere to mutate and adapt to generative language without total functional loss.

2. Sensory-Motor Specialization & Saccades

Reaching is bilaterally accessible; grasping is strictly contralateral. Saccadic eye movements remain integrated subcortically, allowing both hemispheres to initiate and track saccades.

3. Double Memory Dissociation

Left hemisphere handles elaborative semantic memory (prone to schema-based false alarms); right hemisphere maintains literal, veridical episodic memory (highly accurate).
